About the photograph

An original photograph by Alan and Carolina, made to look three-dimensional: the near and far parts of the scene move against each other as the view sways. A striped hoverfly resting on delicate white spring flowers against a soft, blurred green garden background.

How it works

Imaging software estimates how far away every part of the original photo is, and records it as a depth map. The scene is then redrawn from slightly shifted viewpoints, so near things move against the distance just as they do when you shift your head. Edges that a nearer object hides are filled in to match.

What you receive
  • The 3D video (MP4): Plays on any phone, tablet, computer or TV. It loops seamlessly: set it to repeat, use it as a screensaver, or show it on digital frames that play video clips.
  • The red-cyan picture (JPG): Look at it through red/cyan 3D glasses, red over the left eye: on screen, or printed up to 4 × 4 inches (10 × 10 cm). The glasses cost a few dollars.

  • The 3D model (GLB): Opens in 3D viewers and on most phones (iPhone: Files or AR viewers; Windows: 3D Viewer; or any web glTF viewer). Turn it with a finger or the mouse.
